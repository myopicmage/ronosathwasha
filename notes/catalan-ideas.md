# Mine Catalan for ideas

Status: research lead, not canon.

Catalan has several mechanisms worth examining for pressure-tested ideas Rono
might reuse, transform, or deliberately reject. The goal is not to make Rono
resemble Catalan. It is to look for distinctions that another language found
important enough to grammaticalize, then ask whether Rono has the same pressure.

Promising seams:

- the locative and partitive clitics `hi` and `en`, including what they let a
  speaker omit while keeping a referent recoverable;
- clitic placement and ordering, especially how several small grammatical jobs
  compose around one predicate;
- personal articles before names, and whether names benefit from grammar that
  ordinary nouns do not receive;
- `haver-hi` as an existential construction built with a locative element;
- the periphrastic past built from a form of `anar`, "go", plus an infinitive;
- demonstrative variation across Catalan varieties, including systems that keep
  a three-way proximal, medial, and distal contrast;
- vowel reduction and schwa, especially where spelling preserves a distinction
  that ordinary pronunciation neutralizes;
- the division of work among copular and possessive constructions.

For each candidate, record:

1. What communicative pressure the Catalan mechanism solves.
2. Whether Rono already solves that pressure elsewhere.
3. What the mechanism would cost in ambiguity, morphology, and learnability.
4. Whether a Rono-shaped answer falls out of existing machinery.

No Catalan form or distinction becomes Rono merely because it is interesting.
The useful result may be a new feature, evidence for an existing one, or a
clear reason not to add anything.
